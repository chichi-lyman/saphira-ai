# Saphira AI Revenue Engine V1 — 7-Day Market Validation

**Owner:** Chelsea Megan Woods  
**Offer under test:** $35 Automated DM-to-Lead Digital Vault  
**Primary CTA:** Comment or DM CHICHI  
**Validation mode:** Organic demand test; manual fulfillment for first orders  
**Status:** Experiment plan — results not yet established

## 1. Hypothesis

A clearly defined, practical digital product that helps creators and small businesses organize an inbound DM-to-lead workflow can attract qualified interest and paid purchases at a $35 test price.

This is a hypothesis, not a proven market fact. Views, likes, comments, and keyword DMs measure attention or intent signals; completed, non-refunded purchases are the strongest evidence of paid demand.

## 2. Product definition

Do not sell a broad generic prompt collection. Test one specific outcome:

**Automated DM-to-Lead Funnel Setup Vault**

A practical downloadable kit containing:
- A simple DM-to-lead funnel map.
- CHICHI keyword-response copy templates.
- Lead qualification questions and routing rules.
- A basic CRM/lead tracker template.
- A manual-first setup checklist.
- Webhook and automation planning examples, clearly marked as templates rather than preconfigured integrations.
- A troubleshooting and measurement checklist.

State exactly what the buyer receives and what is not included. Do not imply that buying a PDF installs live automations.

**Test variable:** Demonstration-led hook.

## 4. Seven-day operating schedule

| Day | Action | Required record |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Publish Video A; respond to genuine comments | Views, reach, comments, keyword DMs |
| 2 | Review questions and objections; refine response copy | Qualified inquiries and objection themes |
| 3 | Publish Video B; share a labeled demo | DMs, link requests, demo questions |
| 4 | Put the product page/checkout in front of qualified prospects | Unique landing sessions and checkout starts |
| 5 | Publish Video C with transparent price and contents | Paid orders, conversion, refunds |
| 6 | Manually fulfill verified purchases; ask for feedback | Delivery success and customer feedback |
| 7 | Reconcile funnel metrics and decide next experiment | Go, revise, or stop decision |

If a storefront and payment flow are not ready, use a waitlist or interest form and clearly state that the product is not yet available. Do not collect payment until the product, terms, checkout, and delivery path are ready.

## 5. Funnel definitions and metrics

Record unique people where possible, not raw event totals alone.

- **Reach:** unique accounts reached by content.
- **Engaged viewers:** viewers who comment, save, share, or otherwise engage.
- **Keyword responders:** unique people who comment or DM CHICHI.
- **Qualified inquiries:** people who describe a relevant problem and request details.
- **DM-to-click rate:** unique storefront link clickers ÷ unique keyword responders who received the link.
- **Storefront purchase conversion:** unique paid orders ÷ unique storefront visitors.
- **Checkout completion:** paid orders ÷ checkout starts.
- **Refund rate:** refunded orders ÷ paid orders.
- **Service inquiry rate:** unique implementation inquiries ÷ vault buyers.
- **Net receipts:** captured payments minus refunds, payment fees, and applicable chargebacks (tax treatment tracked separately).

Suggested initial directional ranges—not guarantees or universal industry benchmarks:
- DM-to-click: 20%–30%.
- Storefront purchase conversion: 2%–5%.
- Implementation inquiries: 1%–3% of buyers.

These ranges are hypotheses for diagnosing this specific funnel. Interpret them alongside sample size, traffic source, audience fit, and actual purchase counts.

## 6. Event ledger

Maintain one row per event or a privacy-conscious aggregate by day. Suggested columns:

date, content_id, platform, reach, profile_visits, comments, unique_keyword_responders, qualified_inquiries, unique_link_clickers, landing_sessions, checkout_starts, paid_orders, gross_sales, refunds, fees, net_receipts, service_inquiries, delivery_failures, notes.

Use tracked links or platform analytics to avoid guessing clicks. Deduplicate by a privacy-appropriate customer/session identifier. Do not collect sensitive personal information that is unnecessary for fulfillment.

## 7. Manual-first operations

For the first 5–10 paid orders:
1. Confirm payment in the payment provider's dashboard or verified event record.
2. Match the order to the product and customer contact.
3. Deliver the correct asset manually using a controlled, expiring link where available.
4. Record fulfillment status and timestamp.
5. Send a transactional delivery confirmation.
6. Ask for optional product feedback.
7. Record refund/support requests and reasons.

Never fulfill from a screenshot, a social DM claim, or a browser redirect alone. Do not put payment secrets or customer data in a public spreadsheet.

Only automate after the manual process is repeatable and edge cases are understood.

## 8. Go / revise / stop decision

### Go
Proceed to automate the one complete wire when:
- There are genuine paid orders from the intended buyer segment.
- Buyers understand what the product is and is not.
- Delivery is reliable.
- Refunds and support issues are manageable.
- The observed acquisition effort and net receipts support a plausible path to sustainable economics.

### Revise
Change one variable at a time if:
- People respond but do not click: revise CTA, response clarity, or audience fit.
- People click but do not buy: inspect product promise, proof, price, page clarity, and checkout friction.
- Buyers purchase but are disappointed: improve product quality, scope, onboarding, or refund clarity.
- Interest is mostly from peers rather than target buyers: adjust audience and distribution.

### Stop or reposition
Do not build heavy infrastructure if repeated, adequately distributed tests produce no qualified interest or paid demand. Interview target buyers and change the problem/offer before spending more on automation.

A seven-day test with small reach or very few visitors is inconclusive, not proof of no demand.

## 9. One-complete-wire acceptance test

After demand is demonstrated, test this exact sequence in a controlled environment:

Content → permitted DM keyword trigger → product page → hosted checkout → verified payment event → order record → manual/controlled asset delivery → delivery confirmation.

Acceptance requirements:
- Test purchase reconciles to one order.
- Invalid payment webhook signatures are rejected.
- Duplicate events do not create duplicate orders or fulfillment.
- Payment status is verified server-side.
- Delivery failures are visible and safely retryable.
- Customer data and download access are protected.
- Marketing consent is separate from transactional fulfillment.
- Refund and support paths are documented.

## 10. Guardrails

- Follow each platform's current messaging, automation, and disclosure rules.
- Do not send unsolicited bulk DMs or imply personal replies are human-written if they are automated.
- Honor opt-outs and marketing-consent requirements.
- Be transparent about price, contents, limitations, refund terms, and whether a workflow is a live integration or a demonstration.
- Do not claim 50–100 DMs prove demand; use them as a leading indicator only.
- Do not describe one-time vault revenue as monthly recurring revenue.
- Do not claim the experiment succeeded until actual data has been recorded and reconciled.

**Decision principle:** Validate the problem, then the offer, then paid conversion, then reliable fulfillment. Automate only after the manual process works.
