## Purpose

Provide a comprehensive schema of human work — from ancient trades to emerging technology roles — including execution workflows, toolsets, terminology and cognitive frameworks required for competent performance.

## Historical to Emerging Work Spectrum

- **Ancient & traditional** — artisanal crafts, agrarian labour, hunting/gathering specialisations, early administrative and priestly roles
- **Industrial & modern** — manufacturing, logistics, clerical, professional services (law, medicine, engineering, education)
- **Contemporary knowledge & service** — software, design, healthcare specialties, finance, creative industries
- **Frontier & emerging** — AI systems architecture, machine-learning engineering, quantum computing roles, MLOps, AI ethics/safety, neurotechnology, climate engineering, advanced robotics, synthetic biology
- **Specialised manual & hybrid** — precision trades, care work, skilled construction, experimental laboratory support

Existing detailed coverage of AI and quantum roles (see `docs/AI_CAREERS_AND_COMPENSATION.md`, `docs/QUANTUM_COMPUTING_ROLES.md`, `docs/STAGE_TO_ROLE_MAPPING.md`) is treated as a high-priority sub-tree within this domain.

## Execution Workflows Layer

For each major role family the schema targets:

- Standard operating procedures and typical task sequences
- Required toolsets (physical, digital, cognitive)
- Domain-specific terminology and conceptual models
- Specialised cognitive frameworks and decision heuristics
- Entry pathways, credentialing and progression patterns
- Interfaces with other roles and organisational structures

## Mastery Pathway

1. High-level occupational taxonomies (e.g., aligned with major classification systems such as ISCO / SOC concepts)
2. Deep coverage of high-demand contemporary and frontier roles (especially AI, quantum, ethics, systems architecture)
3. Progressive expansion into traditional, industrial and specialised manual roles
4. Workflow and cognitive-framework detail sufficient to support accurate guidance, training outlines and cross-role synthesis

## Usage Notes

When advising on careers or describing work, prefer accurate role definitions, realistic skill requirements and clear distinction between typical and exceptional performance. Cross-link to Economic domain (industry context) and Anthropological domain (cultural meaning of work).
