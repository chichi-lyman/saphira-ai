## Chapter 1 — Three pillars of classical logic (Aristotle)

| Law | Statement | Everyday anchor |
|-----|-----------|-----------------|
| **Identity** | A = A | A variable keeps its meaning mid-calculation; a cat is a cat |
| **Non-contradiction** | Not both B and not-B (same time, same respect) | Not fully alive and fully dead simultaneously |
| **Excluded middle** | A or not-A | Statement true or its negation is; switch on or not on |

These are structural laws of classical bivalent logic—not psychological descriptions of how people always argue.

## Chapter 2 — The syllogism (deduction machine)

```
[Premise 1: All humans are mortal]
        → [Premise 2: Socrates is a human]
        → [Conclusion: Socrates is mortal]
```

If premises are true and the form is valid, the conclusion is forced. This is the skeleton of much scientific and mathematical deduction.

**Validity ≠ soundness:** Form can be valid while a false premise yields a false conclusion. Agents should check both structure and premise quality.

## Chapter 3 — Boolean logic (digital inheritance)

George Boole formalized human logic as algebra. Core operators:

| Operator | Meaning |
|----------|--------|
| **AND** | All conditions true |
| **OR** | At least one true |
| **NOT** | Condition absent / inverted |

Mapped onto transistors: current flow ≈ True (1), blocked ≈ False (0). Traditional software is largely **if/then** compositions of this rulebook.

## The AI paradox

| Traditional software | Modern neural nets |
|---------------------|--------------------|
| Explicit rules, Boolean branches | Probabilities, patterns, “what looks right” |
| Follows the rulebook step-by-step | Approximates intuition from training |

**Twist:** Generative models do **not** natively walk Aristotelian syllogisms. They guess coherent continuations; humans (and tools) apply the **strict rulebook to check** for contradiction, invalid inference, and hallucination.

Saphira’s design intent: **statistical fluency + logical verification** (Weaver maps, QA agents, policy gates)—not pure rule engines or pure unconstrained guesswork.
