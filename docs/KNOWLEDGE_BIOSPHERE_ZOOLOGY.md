## Purpose

Establish durable taxonomic and ecological schemas covering earthly organisms so that Saphira can reason accurately about living systems, their relationships, behaviours and evolutionary context.

## Taxonomic Indexing Schema

Standard biological hierarchy (Linnaean / modern phylogenetic refinements):

1. Domain (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya)
2. Kingdom
3. Phylum (or Division for plants/fungi)
4. Class
5. Order
6. Family
7. Genus
8. Species (and subspecies / variety where relevant)

Coverage targets: flora, fauna, fungi, protists and microorganisms. Emphasis is placed on:
- Major clades and representative taxa at each rank
- Key morphological and molecular diagnostic characters
- Common and scientific nomenclature
- Conservation status and biogeographic distribution where data permit

## Behavioural & Ecological Dynamics

Complementary knowledge layers:

- **Ethology** — species-typical behaviours, social structures, communication modalities (visual, acoustic, chemical, tactile), learning and culture in non-human animals
- **Habitat niches** — abiotic requirements, micro-habitats, range limits
- **Dietary structures** — trophic levels, feeding strategies, symbiotic and parasitic relationships
- **Communication channels** — signalling systems and their evolutionary function
- **Evolutionary adaptations** — morphological, physiological and behavioural traits shaped by selection pressures
- **Ecosystem interactions** — food webs, keystone species, succession, invasive dynamics, mutualisms

## Mastery Pathway

1. High-level domain/kingdom/phylum maps and representative exemplars
2. Family- and genus-level coverage of major vertebrate, invertebrate, plant and fungal groups
3. Species-level depth for organisms of high human relevance (agriculture, medicine, conservation, companion animals, model organisms)
4. Continuous integration of peer-reviewed updates on taxonomy, behaviour and ecology

## Usage Notes

When answering biological or ecological queries, prefer scientific names alongside common names, note taxonomic rank, and distinguish established knowledge from provisional or contested classifications. Cross-link to human uses (agriculture, medicine, culture) when relevant via the Anthropological and Economic domains.
