## Start here: the outcome

This vault helps a creator, solo founder, or small business owner build a simple path from a public call to action to a qualified, trackable sales conversation.

The workflow:

1. Publish useful content that names one customer problem.
2. Invite interested people to comment or message a keyword.
3. Reply transparently and provide the promised resource or product link.
4. Let the prospect choose whether to visit the offer page.
5. Capture only the information needed to respond.
6. Record the lead and next action in a simple tracker.
7. Follow up only where the person has requested or permitted it.
8. Review the numbers weekly and improve the weakest stage.

**The operating principle:** automate repetition only after the manual process is clear, lawful, and working.

## 1. Define the offer before building the funnel

Complete this one-page offer brief.

| Field | Your decision |
|---|---|
| Ideal buyer | A specific type of person with a specific problem |
| Pain point | What is currently slow, confusing, expensive, or being missed? |
| Desired outcome | What practical improvement can the buyer reasonably achieve? |
| Deliverable | The exact guide, template, service, or software being sold |
| Price | One-time or recurring; state it plainly |
| Delivery | Download, appointment, service onboarding, or access |
| Exclusions | What is not included? |
| Support | Channel, response window, and support period |
| Refund terms | Plain-language policy consistent with applicable law |
| Next step | One action the buyer should take |

**Example offer for this vault:** “A downloadable guide with a lead-tracking sheet, message templates, qualification questions, and a manual-first setup checklist.” It does not include account setup or managed automation.

### Offer clarity test

A buyer should be able to answer these questions before paying:
- What exactly do I receive?
- Is it a download or a service?
- Is the price one-time or recurring?
- Does it require a particular platform or paid subscription?
- What is not included?
- Where do I get help?
- What happens if I request a refund?

If the answer is vague, fix the offer page before publishing.

## 2. Map the customer journey

Use this as the minimum viable funnel:

**Content → keyword response → offer page → checkout → delivery → optional service inquiry**

At each stage, define the event you can observe.

| Stage | Event to record | Failure to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Reach, profile visits, comments | Content attracts the wrong audience |
| Keyword | Unique people who ask for CHICHI | CTA unclear or hard to act on |
| Response | Link sent after a valid request | Slow or inconsistent reply |
| Offer page | Unique visits | Offer mismatch or unclear scope |
| Checkout | Checkout started and paid | Friction, trust, payment errors |
| Delivery | Asset delivered and confirmed | Wrong file, broken link, delay |
| Service inquiry | Qualified implementation request | No fit, unclear service scope |

Never count a comment, a link click, or a checkout start as a sale. A sale is a verified paid order, net of refunds and fees in financial reporting.

## 3. Content-to-keyword message templates

Use one CTA consistently during a test. The keyword in this campaign is **CHICHI**.

### Public CTA
“If you want the checklist I use to map a simple lead follow-up workflow, comment **CHICHI** and I’ll send you the details.”

### First response (manual)
“Thanks for asking for CHICHI. Here’s the page with the exact contents, price, and what’s included: [INSERT VERIFIED PRODUCT URL]. It’s a downloadable guide, not a done-for-you installation. If you have a question before deciding, reply here.”

### DM when the person asks what it is
“It’s a practical setup vault: funnel map, response scripts, lead tracker, qualification questions, and a step-by-step manual-first checklist. It helps you organize the process; it does not connect your accounts or run messages automatically.”

### Price question
“The guide is $35 one time. You receive the downloadable materials listed on the page. There is no recurring charge for the vault.”

### “Will this automate my DMs?”
“Not by itself. The vault explains how to design the workflow and gives you reusable templates. Platform automation requires separate account setup, permissions, and sometimes a paid third-party plan.”

### “Can you set it up for me?”
“I also offer scoped implementation for businesses that need the workflow configured. I’ll ask a few questions about your platform, lead volume, tools, and handoff requirements, then confirm whether there’s a fit and provide a written scope and quote. No work begins until you approve it.”

### Not a fit / no pressure
“Thanks for checking it out. If the guide isn’t what you need right now, no pressure. I won’t keep messaging you about it.”

### Follow-up only when requested or permitted
“Quick check-in on the guide you asked about: do you still want the link, or should I close the loop? Either answer is completely fine.”

Do not send repeated unsolicited promotional messages. Respect platform rules, opt-outs, and applicable messaging/marketing laws. Do not scrape contacts or add people to email/SMS marketing lists without the required consent.

## 4. Lead qualification: keep it lightweight

Ask only what is needed to determine the next helpful step.

1. What do you sell, and who do you sell it to?
2. Where do leads currently come from?
3. Which platform receives the inquiries?
4. What happens after someone expresses interest?
5. What is the biggest bottleneck: slow replies, missed follow-up, poor qualification, scheduling, or reporting?
6. Are you looking for a DIY guide or a configured implementation?

### Simple routing rules

- **DIY / early stage:** share the guide page; no pressure.
- **Implementation interest:** ask the six questions; record consent and requested contact method; schedule a human review.
- **Not a fit:** explain why and close politely.
- **Sensitive or payment issue:** route to a human; never request card numbers, passwords, or authentication codes in DMs.
- **Refund, contract, discount, or complaint:** human review; do not let an unreviewed bot make a binding decision.

## 5. Lead tracker template

Create one row per person or business, using a stable internal lead ID. Avoid storing sensitive personal data.

| Field | Example / allowed values |
|---|---|
| Lead ID | L-0001 |
| Date received | YYYY-MM-DD |
| Source | TikTok / Instagram / YouTube / referral |
| Campaign | RE-V1-DAY-01 |
| Keyword requested | CHICHI |
| Business type | Creator / local service / consultant |
| Need | Follow-up / qualification / booking / reporting |
| Intent | DIY / implementation / unclear |
| Permission | Asked for link / service reply requested / marketing opt-in / none |
| Stage | New / replied / qualified / link sent / won / closed |
| Next action | Send requested link / human review / close |
| Next action date | YYYY-MM-DD |
| Outcome | No response / not fit / paid / proposal / retained |
| Notes | Minimal, factual, non-sensitive |

**Data hygiene:** restrict access, use unique IDs, set a retention period, delete records when no longer needed, and do not put passwords, payment details, health data, or private message dumps in the tracker.

## 7. Automation design: what to automate and what to keep human

| Workflow | V1 mode | Later automation gate |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword acknowledgement | Manual | Platform-supported trigger, explicit request, rate limits |
| Product link response | Manual | Approved template and channel permission |
| Lead record | Manual sheet/CRM | Deduplication, access controls, retention |
| Payment verification | Provider dashboard/test mode | Verified webhook signature + idempotency |
| Digital delivery | Storefront/manual | Secure expiring link, retry and failure alert |
| Service qualification | Human review | Structured intake with clear disclosure |
| Proposal / contract | Human | Never auto-send binding terms without approval |
| Refund / discount | Human | Human authorization and audit trail |
| Marketing follow-up | Separate opt-in | Consent log, unsubscribe, suppression list |

### Technical acceptance criteria for a future integration
- Server-side secrets only; never expose API keys in browser code.
- Verify webhook signatures before processing events.
- Make payment processing idempotent; duplicate events must not create duplicate orders or deliveries.
- Store minimum necessary data with tenant isolation and least-privilege access.
- Log action IDs and status, not secrets or unnecessary message content.
- Use retry limits, dead-letter handling, and operator alerts.
- Secure download URLs; avoid permanent public links to paid files.
- Separate transactional delivery from optional marketing consent.
- Human approval for refunds, contracts, discounts, mass messaging, and consequential actions.
- Test success, failure, duplicate event, timeout, and retry paths before production.

## 8. Seven-day demand validation sprint

The goal is to learn whether the intended buyer understands the offer and will pay for it—not to manufacture impressive engagement numbers.

### Day 1 — Problem recognition
Publish: “If people are commenting on your content but you’re still manually chasing every lead, your content may not be the problem. Your follow-up process may be. Comment CHICHI if you want the workflow checklist.”

### Day 2 — Listen
Review replies and questions. Record the exact words people use. Do not change the offer based on one comment.

### Day 3 — Demonstration
Show a clearly labeled example: keyword request → transparent response → offer page → lead tracker. State that it is a demonstration, not a live customer system.

### Day 4 — Direct offer test
Share the actual product preview, price, deliverables, and exclusions. Invite questions; do not pressure people.

### Day 5 — Objection content
Answer the most common real question: “Does this install the automation for me?” Explain the guide versus implementation service distinction.

### Day 6 — Fulfill and learn
For verified purchases, deliver the file, record support issues, and ask for feedback. Do not invent testimonials or imply unearned outcomes.

### Day 7 — Review
Calculate the metrics below. Choose GO, REVISE, or STOP based on evidence and sample size.

## 9. Metrics and formulas

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| DM-to-click rate | Unique link clickers ÷ unique keyword responders |
| Product-page conversion | Verified paid orders ÷ unique product-page visitors |
| Checkout completion | Verified paid orders ÷ checkout starts |
| Refund rate | Refunded orders ÷ paid orders |
| Service inquiry rate | Qualified implementation inquiries ÷ buyers |
| Net product receipts | Captured product payments − refunds − payment fees − chargebacks |
| MRR | Sum of active recurring contract value for the period; exclude one-time vault sales |

Track each content post separately. Deduplicate people where possible. Label estimates and incomplete attribution.

### Directional test signals (not industry benchmarks)
- DM-to-click: 20–30% may be a useful initial hypothesis.
- Product-page conversion: 2–5% may be a useful initial hypothesis.
- Implementation inquiries: 1–3% of buyers may be a useful initial hypothesis.

These are planning thresholds, not promises. A tiny sample cannot prove or disprove demand.

### Decision rules
- **GO:** intended buyers make verified purchases, understand what they bought, receive it reliably, and support/refund load is manageable.
- **REVISE:** people ask but do not click → clarify CTA/response; people click but do not buy → improve page, trust, scope, or checkout; buyers are disappointed → fix product/expectations before more promotion.
- **STOP / reposition:** after multiple adequately distributed tests, qualified buyers show little interest or willingness to pay. Interview the audience and test a different problem before investing in more infrastructure.

## 10. Implementation service: separate offer, separate contract

The vault is self-serve education. A configured Saphira AI implementation is a different service.

A paid implementation engagement should define:
- Business outcome and baseline process.
- Platforms, accounts, permissions, and integrations in scope.
- Number and type of workflows.
- Data sources and retention requirements.
- Human approval points and prohibited actions.
- Monitoring, support window, response times, and change requests.
- Setup fee, recurring fee, billing cadence, cancellation, and third-party costs.
- Acceptance tests and handover.
- Data-processing and confidentiality terms where applicable.

Do not advertise “fully autonomous,” “guaranteed leads,” or guaranteed revenue. Quote only after discovery and a written scope. A $1,000–$2,500 monthly range is a planning hypothesis, not a published promise or universal price.
